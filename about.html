Research

My primary research estimates how AI infrastructure investment is reshaping regional electricity demand and whether grid planning tools are built to see it coming. AI Infrastructure and Regional Electricity Demand: Evidence from U.S. Interconnection Queues estimates a 34.8 index-point divergence in ERCOT minimum hourly demand above a synthetic counterfactual, using a four-layer identification strategy across PJM, ERCOT, and MISO. The companion policy brief argues FERC should require grid operators to publish large load interconnection queue data on the same terms as generation, tracing thirty years of precedent and proposing a specific mechanism that does not require new legislation. Replication materials are archived on Harvard Dataverse.
